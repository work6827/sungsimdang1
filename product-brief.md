# Sungsimdang Passport — Know Before You Go

Team Avengers · Dongguk University · Enterprise 4.0 and Business Model Innovation

## Company and Problem
Sungsimdang is a Daejeon bakery. Its official site describes its local roots and role in the city. The business hypothesis is that visitors may spend time and money traveling without knowing whether their first-choice product will remain available. The proposal also addresses resale and one-time tourism. The size and frequency of these problems have not been measured by this team.

## Solution and Differentiation
“Know Before You Go.” “We keep the queue and remove the wasted trip.” The system supports better information and fair access; it does not eliminate scarcity or sell premium queue access. The extension connects a staff AI-glasses workflow, forecasting and multilingual guidance. Bread Pass and Pilgrim Ledger remain central parts of the original concept.

## Customer Experience
Observe and confirm inventory → compare product/store/time forecasts → ask the assistant → reserve an eligible item → verify pickup → collect one stamp → unlock a proposed city reward. Visitors can also use information without making a reservation.

## Business Model and Competitors
Proposed value: better allocation, fewer wasted trips, less resale opportunity, repeat visits and tourism engagement. Possible revenue comes from B2B software licensing and tourism partnerships, not customer premium queue-jumping. Alternatives include official product pages, calls to stores, social updates, generic booking tools and ordinary loyalty systems. No comparative superiority is proven. Sungsimdang is a proposed first customer; Daejeon City and KORAIL are prospective partners, not confirmed agreements. Aggregated visitor insights would require appropriate privacy controls.

## Global Application and Website MVP
Daejeon → other Korean destination bakeries → Kyoto/Japan → Europe. The operating software can travel; bakery products remain locally sold under the proposal. Expansion needs local language, accessibility, employee acceptance, data protection, hardware cost and operating-model validation. MVP: three stores, twelve products, three visitors, nine routes including the retained How It Works page. It uses browser-local data and no paid service.

## Validation and Success Measures
Preserve the proposed two-week, two-store pilot. Compare with/without forecasts; observe resale listings; use exit surveys; follow an opt-in panel for 60 days; interview 20 local regulars; assess whether tourist allocations displace residents. Targets: 30% fewer first-choice misses, 50% fewer observed resale listings, 15 percentage points higher return intent. These are targets, not achieved results.
