# Customer and operational research plan

## Feasible two-week practicum
Recruit 8–12 exploratory participants: aim for 3 local regulars, 3 domestic tourists, 2 international visitors, and 2 limited-product/enthusiast visitors (categories may overlap; record overlap). If access is granted, add 2 staff and 1 manager conversations; do not imply access exists. Run a short survey with approximately 20–30 respondents if feasible. This is exploratory convenience sampling, not representative estimation.

Preserve the original future pilot separately: 20 local regular interviews, two pilot stores, two-week comparison and opt-in 60-day follow-up. Student research does not replace this pilot.

Week 1: consent, secondary research, recruitment, exploratory interviews and observation planning; synthesize hypotheses. Week 2: 5–8 usability sessions, issue fixes, survey if feasible, final evidence review. Do not record customers or staff without permission. Use participant codes; avoid collecting phone numbers in research notes; explain voluntary participation and deletion process.

## Visitor interview guide — 15–20 minutes
1. Tell me about your last bakery visit.
2. What did you hope to buy? Why that item?
3. How did you plan the store, timing and transport?
4. What information did you check? What was difficult to find?
5. What happened when you arrived? Describe the queue and product availability.
6. Have you missed a product you wanted? What did you do next?
7. What information, if any, would have changed your plan?
8. How do you decide whether information is trustworthy?
9. Tell me about any reservation system you have used. What worked or did not?
10. What would feel fair or unfair when a limited product is allocated?
11. What did you do elsewhere in the city? What might make you return?
12. What language or accessibility issues did you encounter?
Show the prototype only after behavior questions. Ask what the participant believes each estimate and pass means; do not ask “Wouldn’t this app help?”

## Staff interview guide
Describe a recent restocking cycle. How do you know what is on a shelf versus in storage? How are shortages communicated? Which task takes repeated effort? What happens when similar products are mixed? When would a camera obstruct service or create concern? How would you want to correct an incorrect count? What information should never be recorded? Do not assume existing procedures or permission to wear glasses.

## Manager interview guide
Walk through a recent production decision. Which records informed it? How are stores compared? What changed when demand was unusual? What would make a recommendation actionable? What error would be costly? What integration, consent or staff approval would be required? What allocation would be fair to regulars? Avoid asking for confidential records unless separately authorized.

## Short survey
Use neutral wording and optional demographic questions. Ask last-visit date bracket; resident/tourist category; first-choice item found (yes/no/not planned); approximate wait bracket; information sources used; whether plans changed due to availability; main difficulty (including none); clarity/trust of a shown forecast on a 1–5 scale with “unsure”; fairness concerns; optional return intent. Separate pre-prototype behavior from post-prototype opinion. Report denominator and missing responses. Do not generalize a convenience sample to all visitors.

## Observation checklist
With permission: store/period; queue start/end observations without identifying people; shelf visibility/overlap/lighting; staff restock signals; what information is publicly displayed; obstacles to accessible viewing; inventory correction steps/time. Never infer total inventory from the shelf. Do not film faces, payment terminals, receipts or screens.
